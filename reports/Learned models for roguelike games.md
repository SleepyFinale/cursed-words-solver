# Stop fine-tuning moves; search whole runs

Keep the rules player and the override model, stop expecting one-move overrides to produce a strong player, and spend the next **85-210k runs (about 1-4 weeks of machine time and two weeks of development)** on whole-run policy changes judged by a rebuilt benchmark. The order is: fix the benchmark, re-profile, test how hard a plan should commit, tune the rules player with a factorial design and then evolution strategies, and only then refit a plan-aware override on the new rules. Strong agents in roguelikes and similar games keep a fixed tactical engine, add hand-chosen strategy structure, and learn a narrow layer of run-level choices. No documented project has learned a whole game of this kind at this budget and beaten a good rules player, and the one respectable result with both layers learned withholds its budget. Our own record agrees: the champion's +0.152 shrinks to about +0.09 to +0.13, wins sit level at 190 vs 190, and ten 1,024-run training iterations before it promoted nothing. With 74% of losses at stages 4-5, the gap looks like builds that do not scale, and labels that change one move under the rules player's continuation cannot value a coordinated build. **The 2,464-run plan screen is still running and its outcome is pending**, so no step below depends on what it has shown so far. No source tells us how much headroom exists. My judgement is +0.2 to +0.8 outcome from tuning alone, and more only if new plan features work, so every step carries a result that stops it.

Read together, five behaviours recur. First, **the tactical engine stays fixed**. sts-ironclad-agent keeps combat search as its one classical part, and sts-rl-agent reports six failed attempts to replace search with a learned combat model. Your own test points the same way: three times the search effort at bosses scored −0.01 (t −0.4), and the project concluded the bottleneck is the build, not word search ([sim-game.md](../docs/game-research/sim-game.md)). So the search-budget lever seen in sts-rl-agent, 4% to 14% wins between 2,000 and 50,000 simulations, does not appear to transfer at bosses.

Second, **strength comes from named strategy structure, not discovered structure**. AlphaStar conditions on a hand-chosen build statistic z because a policy good at one style gets worse under any naive step toward another, and its authors say choosing those statistics took domain knowledge ([Vinyals et al.](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf)). Nachum et al. attribute most of hierarchy's benefit to temporally coherent exploration, on non-game control tasks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10618)). The NetHack organisers give unsolved hierarchy and long-term credit as the reasons rules won.

Third, **direct search over small policies beats value learning when returns are noisy and delayed**. In Tetris, value-based methods reached thousands of lines where noisy cross-entropy reached 350,000 and 35M with better features, and a hand-written rule still beat the 350,000 ([review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.01652)). Features set the ceiling. Texel tuning's largest single gains came from position selection and from exposing parameters to the optimiser, and it cannot learn what the engine lacks ([CPW](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Texel%27s_Tuning_Method)). Evolution-strategy gradients do not gain variance with episode length, unlike policy gradients ([Salimans et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03864)).

Fourth, **they replace the noisy final score when training**. Suphx rewards the change in predicted final outcome per round, and Hearthstone deck search uses health difference rather than win rate to keep the size of wins and losses ([Fontaine et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10656)). Fifth, **they make evaluation cheap with fast simulators and duplicate seeds**. Mortal reports up to 40K hanchans an hour and duplicate sets across seats ([docs](https://mortal.ekyu.moe/), [strength page](https://mortal.ekyu.moe/perf/strength.html)), though its strength against humans is unmeasured. Bigger networks did not break plateaus where tried: balatro-rl's 5.5x network stayed flat, and doubling HiHack's transformer made it worse ([Piterbarg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19240)).

Budget decides which of these you can copy. At 7-12k runs a day, TD-Gammon 0.0's 200k games take 17-29 days and the 1.5M behind later versions take 125-214 days ([secondary table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TD-Gammon)). A 2M-game Hearthstone quality-diversity run takes 167-286 days ([Zhang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.03534)), and 10M 2048 episodes take years ([Jaśkowski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.05085)). The analogies also break in places: you have no self-play curriculum, no human corpus, and a rules player far stronger than stock heuristics. So the sts-ironclad-agent number shows that this architecture can be tested, not that 50% is reachable here. I would not forecast a win rate from it. Its stated speed of about a minute per game per core is roughly 11,500 games a day on 8 cores, the same order as yours, but that shows evaluation is affordable and says nothing about training (Inference).

## Our own runs show the one-move route is nearly spent

The champion is real enough to keep and too small to build on. On 1,024 fresh seeds it gained +0.152 (SE 0.078, t 1.96, 95% interval about 0 to 0.30), and the wins split 62 model-only and 62 rules-only ([sim-game.md](../docs/game-research/sim-game.md), project memory `ai-roadmap.md`). With 124 discordant pairs, the win difference has an SE of about 1.1 points, so **"level" still allows about ±2 win points**. Shrinkage with a prior effect SD of 0.1-0.2 gives +0.09 to +0.13 (Inference). NathaServo supplies two-thirds of the gain, at +1.0 (t 3.4) on 107 runs, and the rest is +0.05 (t 0.67). That split was a look after seeing the data, so it is a lead rather than a finding, and a good first use of a single-use seed block once the benchmark is rebuilt.

Our record also weighs against three natural next moves. **Better models have not shown a play gain:** LightGBM beat ridge offline, 0.19 vs 0.156, yet head-to-head against the linear model it scored −0.005 and +0.03 (t 0.17). That was on 256 seeds each, so a small edge would not show, but twin networks also ranked no better offline. **More of the same data will not fix this:** in the long run, iterations 0-5 all lost their confirmations (paired −0.22 to −0.50) despite out-of-fold penalised gains of +0.09 to +0.13, and iterations 6-9 were level at best. **Repeatable short-horizon labels are not sufficient:** one-stage build strength has split-half correlation 0.67, but the pin rule using it scored −0.05 (t −0.35), although pin side may simply have little headroom, and `build_value`, at CV R² 0.52, lost −0.11. The build-aware boss model reached AUC 0.97 and added only +0.07 (t 0.8). Elsewhere, [balatro_rl](https://github.com/stratosfearwashere-create/balatro_rl) shows the same shape: cloning roughly equals its teacher, PPO adds a little, and wins stay at zero (AR, on an older simulator).

This corrects one inference in the notes, which say the override sits on the tactical layer that other projects found hardest to improve. It does not. `VALUE_KINDS` in `expert.py` is buy, hippo, sell_buy, leave, boss, pin and michael_pick, all run-level, and the model's offline edge is in boss, leave and pin choices rather than buys. So the limit is horizon and coordination, not which layer is learned (Inference). A trial changes one move and lets the rules player continue, so as the roadmap says, the models have never seen a run with a different plan. The AlphaStar example describes the consequence: a new style looks bad until its supporting moves exist. The tabulated losses by stage, the ×3.8 target growth per stage and the human comparison (6 of 12 human runs reached stage 6 vs 37 of 528 after later route settings, with a small sample, a different crown range and a different route policy) all point at scaling, though none proves headroom ([ai-roadmap.md](../docs/game-research/ai-roadmap.md)).

## Most of the rebuild reuses code you already have

**Benchmark (step 2).** `bench.job` plays `apply(g, pol.act(g))` on one shared stream, while `expert.play_out` reseeds before each action from `_point(g)`, the action kind and a count. Move that into `bench.job` behind a spec flag added to `SPEC_KEYS`, about 10 lines, so `bench run` refuses mixed arms. Run the pilot before building the base cache, because keying and each code change invalidate cached runs and `bench.py`'s fingerprint will flag them. This reverses the evaluation notes' order to avoid paying for the base twice. The pilot is `finish_early` off vs 0.75 on 264 seeds per arm, compared with the legacy SD of 2.80, though that figure predates current code. If keying passes, a second stage adds per-event streams (shop slot, grid cell, boss draft, item lineage) and replaces pool-indexed picks with hashed stable-rank or Gumbel-max selection, so an item stays chosen when the pool changes ([Oberst and Sontag](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/oberst19a.html), [Buffalo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11084v1)). `rng.range(0, len(...))` appears 62 times across nine `sim/game` modules, so budget 2-3 more days for this stage. Never put gold, score or hand contents in a key.

`bench.compare` then gains a sequential test on complete 88-seed blocks, in the Brownian form fishtest uses ([Fishtest](https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html)): LLR = (δ/var) × (S − nδ/2), accepting at 2.77 and rejecting at −1.56, with a cap at 1.5x the fixed n. The notes' simulation on real `e5` differences gave 5.3% false positives, 84% power at +0.2 and savings of 20-77%, and pentanomial modelling adds nothing because the paired variance already captures it. With a cached base of 2,000 seeds, the estimated cost of a +0.2 comparison falls from about 5.4 hours to 1-2 hours at SD 2.5. The gate is that test on outcome, plus a lower bound on win difference above −2 points, plus a separate pass or fail on Michael. Search on the 7M block, treat the 8M block as regression only, and promote only on a block used once, such as 9M + 10,000 × k. Each promotion reports raw mean and t, seeds, candidates searched, shrunk estimate, stratified bootstrap interval and the fresh-seed result. I would not adopt IQM, because trimming 25% removes the wins you care about.

**Commitment (step 4).** Today `_build_plan_step` refuses off-plan items, restocks up to twice per shop and keeps $5. Replace the refusal with a plan score bonus w in {0, small, medium, hard} and a commit time in {first shop, end of stage 1, end of stage 2}, keep generic points allowed, and log plan offers seen per shop so a plan's bad luck is not read as weak synergy. Run this on the top four plans by the shrunk screen. Then use the `chartune.py` halving machinery within characters, keeping at least two plans per character through round 2, and confirm each promotion on 578 fresh runs (Δ 0.7). A shared model with character and band as inputs replaces per-cell conclusions: a cell's six benchmark seeds give an SE of 1.14, and `swap_sell` shows the risk, since `chartune` promoted it and it then scored −0.20 on the benchmark. Under the notes' guessed variances, a global plan delivers most of the gain, and per-character choice adds about 0.2-0.3.

**Tuning (steps 5-6).** Pick 20-40 of the 57 `PowerConfig` fields plus item-family multipliers and set each ±1 at random per run. Regress outcome on the factors with cell effects, using the existing `with_overrides` path. Main-effect SE is 2σ/√N, so **20k runs give about ±0.04-0.06 on every factor at once**, against `chartune`'s detection limit of about +0.7 on 96 runs ([NIST](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri3331.htm)). It measures whole-run effects, so downstream consequences are included. Rarely triggered knobs will dilute, and interactions need a follow-up round, so the final test is the combined setting on a single-use block. Evolution strategies follow only with new plan features (owned tag count, generators owned, scaling items, stage), because Tetris shows features set the ceiling. The notes' toy simulation is a best case on a smooth landscape: 10 parameters is workable in 1-3 days, 30 needs 3-11 days, and 100 is out of reach. Plain cross-entropy at low headroom moved backwards by selecting on noise, so gate every 5-10k runs on fresh seeds and rotate seeds between generations ([Strens and Moore](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v3/strens02a.html)).

**Override refit (steps 7-8).** Randomise the plan per run, pass it to the RCT learner as a feature, and collect trials under the new rules. This is a tree version of a universal value function ([Schaul et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/schaul15.html)). Compare the current λ label with `play_out(stages=1)` labels. Keep LightGBM or ridge. The notes' settings are learning rate at or below 0.02, 8-16 leaves, at least 500-2,000 rows per leaf and folds grouped by run, since 76k labels support only about 50-190 independent effects at σ 4, or roughly 160-630 at the measured label SD of 2.2 (my rescaling). Before promotion, run the decile calibration from the notes: on fresh seeds, randomly apply or withhold the model's overrides, and fit realised against predicted gain. That takes about 512 runs. Also run a dose test at 1, 3 and 10 overrides per run, since a flat curve reveals interactions directly. The plan picker then fits on the same logs. The notes' rough, unsimulated guess is 30-60k logged runs.

**Speed and restarts (steps 3 and 9).** `engine/search.py` is 1,481 lines of mostly Python with some NumPy, which closes one gap in the notes. By Amdahl's law, a 20x native hot path gives about 3x overall if it is 70% of CPU, and about 7x at 90%. Port only behind differential tests against seeded Python runs, since a port must not change play. For restarts, `clone()` is a deep copy, so saved stage-3 states can be replayed under many variants, aimed at the stages where 74% of losses happen. Use full clones, as `light=True` copies must never play words, and rebind `g.rng`, `g.shop.rng` and `g.encounter.rng` as `play_out` does. Restarts condition on survival, so hold the state mix fixed across arms. Rollouts stay a narrow tool. At σ 4, separating moves 0.5 apart takes about 1,000 playouts, or 12-69 decisions a day, and your pin rollouts agreed on sign only 52.6% of the time between samples. If you pilot them, use boss choice with sequential halving over truncated play-outs. A search on one fixed seed must never drive play, since it sees future shops.

## Every step has a result that stops it

These thresholds are mine, built from the notes' calculations. Set them before the runs, not after.

| Step | Stop or change course if | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Determinism | Any non-zero difference on base vs identical base, one 88-seed block | Halt everything and fix it |
| A/A check | Salted base vs base gives SD outside about 3.1-3.9, or sequential replay gives over about 8% false positives | Do not trust any gate until explained |
| Keyed pilot | SD of differences stays at or above about 2.5 (legacy 2.80, SE about 4%), or keyed base differs from legacy by over 2 SE, or stock and letter frequencies shift | Keep the legacy stream in `bench`, and use keying for labels only |
| Profile | Portable hot path is under about 70% of CPU under `NetPolicy` | No port; pursue cheaper probes only with a benchmark, since they change play |
| Commitment dial | No setting with w above 0 comes within −0.5 of rules across the top plans | Check plan offers per shop once, then drop plan commitment and go to step 5 with scaling features |
| Per-character plans | No plan beats rules by over +0.3 on any character on fresh seeds | Plan choice is not the bottleneck; skip step 8 |
| Factorial | Combined top factors fail the sequential test at +0.2 on a single-use block | Weights have little headroom, which is plausible for a hand-tuned player; skip step 6 unless new features exist |
| Evolution strategies | No fresh-seed gate reaches +0.2 after 20k runs at 30 parameters or fewer | Stop; do not raise the dimension |
| Stage labels | Better offline but no better in play, as with the pin rule | Drop them and keep the λ label |
| Override refit | Calibration slope under about 0.3, a flat or falling dose curve, or failure at +0.2 | Ship the tuned rules, or keep the override only as a conservative margin; do not iterate the loop |
| Plan picker | Does not beat the best fixed per-character table on fresh seeds | Ship the table |
| Whole-run learning pilot | A 15-30k-run scorer pilot ends over 0.5 below the tuned rules | Close this route until the simulator is 10x faster |
| Programme | After about 150k runs, confirmed gain totals under +0.3 and wins are unchanged | Stop learning work; compare human runs for behaviours the rules cannot express, and try coding-agent rule patches through the same gate ([AutoTavern, unverified preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33845), [NetHackers `evolve`, self-reported](https://github.com/dunnolab/nethackers)) |

"Consistently strong" also needs a definition you can test. I propose a first milestone of +1.0 outcome and +2 to +3 win points overall on a single-use block of about 2,000 seeds, with no crown band or character shrunk below −0.2. This is a target, not a forecast. +2 win points takes about 1,900-2,200 seeds at your 12-14% discordance rate. Today's wins run 57%, 36% and 20% at crowns 0, 1 and 2 and 1% at crown 7, and 38% for BonesTheDog down to 7% for WetDennis. Character spread will remain, since it may be partly inherent difficulty. Crown 7 should be judged by stage reached until wins are measurable.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the +0.152 means. It reads less like the first step of a learning curve and more like the plateau of a method: one-move labels tune a player without ever showing it a different way to play. So the next thing to build is a parameterised rules player that can commit to a plan, plus a benchmark that can tell a real +0.2 from a lucky one. Learning returns on top of that, with far more varied data than it has ever had.

The largest unknown is headroom, and steps 4-5 are designed to measure it within about a week of machine time for 40-50k runs. If they find none, that is also decisive: it would mean the gap lies in behaviours the rules cannot yet express, which more training, of any kind, would not reach. The sts-ironclad-agent question is worth settling too, because its `combat_engine` code would show whether the best number in the field used hidden information, and that decides how much the architecture itself deserves the credit.
